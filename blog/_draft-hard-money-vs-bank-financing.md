## Why this article

This is the objection-handling piece. Every investor comparing lenders asks the
same question — "isn't hard money expensive?" — and the honest answer, worked in
dollars, converts far better than a defensive one. It has to be genuinely fair to
bank financing, including the cases where the bank is the right choice.

The homepage comparison table covers this at a glance. This article does the
arithmetic.

## Outline

1. **The question, framed properly**
   Cost of capital is not a rate. It is rate plus points plus time plus the
   probability of losing the deal entirely. Compare all four, not just the first.

2. **The same deal, financed two ways**
   Use the Atlanta case study numbers: $285K purchase, $65K rehab, $480K ARV.

   | | Hard money | Bank investment loan |
   |---|---|---|
   | Basis required at closing | | |
   | Rehab financed | Yes, draw-based | Typically no |
   | Rate | | |
   | Points | | |
   | Time to close | 2–3 weeks (12 days actual) | 45–60 days |
   | Income documentation | None | 2 years returns, W-2s, DTI |
   | Cost over a 6-month hold | | |

   Show the dollar totals and the cash-on-cash return for each, not just the rate.

3. **Where the bank actually wins**
   Long holds, stabilized rentals, a W-2 borrower on their first property, a deal
   with no deadline pressure. Say so plainly.

4. **Where hard money wins**
   - Rehab is financed; the bank makes you carry it
   - Leverage is quoted on total cost, not just purchase price
   - The deal closes before the seller moves on
   - No personal DTI test, no cap on financed properties

5. **The variable nobody prices: the deal you don't get**
   A bank loan you cannot close in time has an infinite cost of capital.
   Quantify it — if one deal in three is lost to a slow close, the expected value
   of the cheaper rate goes negative.

6. **A decision framework**
   Four questions: How fast must this close? Does renovation need financing?
   Will personal income documentation qualify? How long is the hold?

7. **FAQ (5 questions, FAQPage schema)**
   - Is a hard money loan more expensive than a bank loan?
   - Can I use a bank loan for a fix and flip?
   - Why do hard money lenders charge points?
   - Can I refinance a hard money loan into a bank loan later?
   - Which is better for a first-time flipper?
